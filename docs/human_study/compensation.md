# Compensation policy — Phase 12 decision-setting study

**Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 2026-08-05

---

## Principle

Participants are financial-crime professionals giving up several hours of skilled time.
They are paid for it, at a rate that reflects that, and the payment is not contingent on
what they say.

Three things follow, and each rules something out:

**Payment is for time, never for agreement.** No bonus depends on a participant's ratings,
on their agreement with other participants, or on how close they come to any reference.
A payment contingent on agreement buys agreement, and the inter-rater statistic then
measures the incentive.

**Payment is not so large as to be coercive.** The rate is a professional consultancy rate
for the field, not a multiple of it. A payment large enough to override someone's judgement
about whether they want to take part is a consent problem, not a generosity.

**Withdrawal costs nothing.** A participant who stops half way is paid pro rata for the
items they completed. This is stated in the information sheet and the consent form, because
a participant who believes stopping forfeits their payment is not free to stop.

## Rate and structure

| | |
|---|---|
| Basis | Piloted time estimate at a professional hourly rate |
| Estimated workload | ~60 items, 4–6 hours including the 30-minute training pack |
| Rate | [AMOUNT] per hour — set at the going consultancy rate for AML analyst time in [JURISDICTION], to be confirmed against [SOURCE] before submission |
| Fixed payment | Rate × the piloted estimate, paid on completion |
| Training | Paid. It is a required half hour of their time |
| Overrun | If a participant's recorded active time exceeds the estimate by more than 20%, they are paid for the actual time |
| Partial completion | Pro rata by items completed, paid on withdrawal, no questions asked |
| Method | [BANK TRANSFER / VOUCHER], within [N] working days of completion |

**The estimate must be piloted before the rate is fixed.** Two people from the target
population complete ten items each, timed. If the observed per-item time differs materially
from the assumption, the estimate — and therefore the fixed payment — is revised upward
before recruitment opens. Setting a fixed fee against an unpiloted guess is how a study
ends up paying below its stated rate.

## Recording

Payment records are held by [FINANCE/ADMIN FUNCTION], joined to names and bank details, and
**never joined to the research dataset**. The research dataset knows a participant only as
`rater-03`.

Retained for 7 years for financial audit, then destroyed (see the Data Management Plan §4).

## Tax

Payments may be taxable income for the recipient. Participants are told this in the
recruitment material and advised that they are responsible for their own declaration.
[INSTITUTION] does not provide tax advice.

## If the internal-review fallback is used instead

If the study runs under the internal governance model in `fallback_governance.md`, the
reviewers are colleagues acting within their normal role and are **not** paid a
participation fee. That difference is one more reason the fallback is a weaker instrument,
and it is stated in the paper alongside the others: an internal reviewer who is a colleague
of the author is not an independent judge in the way a paid external professional is.
