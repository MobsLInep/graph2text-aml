# Participant information sheet

**Study:** Decision-setting evaluation of generated Suspicious Activity Report narratives
**Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 2026-08-05

You are invited to take part in a research study. Before you decide, please read this
sheet. Ask us anything that is unclear. Take as long as you need.

---

## What is this study about?

Banks and other regulated firms must file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) when they
identify potential money laundering. Writing the narrative part of a SAR is slow manual
work. We have built software that drafts that narrative automatically from transaction
data.

Automatic scoring can tell us whether a draft is grammatical or whether it repeats the
right numbers. It cannot tell us whether the draft is any **use to an investigator**. That
is what we are asking you.

## Why me?

Because you have working knowledge of anti-money-laundering practice. We are recruiting
6–10 people with that background. We are deliberately not using general crowdworkers: the
judgement we need — is this the right regulatory register, would this survive review — is
professional judgement.

## Do I have to take part?

No. Participation is entirely voluntary. If you decide to take part you can stop at any
time, without giving a reason. Stopping has no consequence of any kind, and you are paid
for the work you have already done.

## What would I actually do?

You will be given a link and a personal identifier. For each item you will see:

- a picture of a transaction network,
- a structured summary of the facts of that case,
- **one** draft narrative about it.

You will:

1. Read the draft and rate it on five scales (1–7): factual correctness, completeness,
   actionability, readability and professional register, and regulatory tone.
2. Answer one yes/no question: would you file this after review?
3. Edit the draft in a text box until you would be willing to file it.

The software records how long you spend and what you changed.

**Roughly 60 items, about 4–6 hours in total.** It is entirely self-paced. Your progress
saves automatically, you can close the window whenever you like and pick up where you left
off, and we suggest sessions of no more than an hour.

## Which system wrote each draft?

**We will not tell you, and this is deliberate.** The drafts come from several different
systems, including a simple template baseline. If you knew which was which, your ratings
would be influenced by that knowledge rather than by the text.

We are telling you now that this information is being withheld, and why. You will be told
which systems were compared once the study closes, if you would like to know.

This is the only thing about the study that is withheld from you. Nothing you are told is
untrue.

## Are the cases real?

**No.** Every case is synthetic or anonymised public data. They come from a published
research dataset of *simulated* transactions (IBM AMLworld) and, possibly, anonymised
public blockchain data (Elliptic2). There is no real customer, no real SAR and no real
identifiable person anywhere in what you will see. We could not show you a real SAR: they
are confidential by statute.

## Am I being tested?

No. **The software is under evaluation, not you.** We do not compute or report any measure
of any individual participant's performance. We do check whether the panel as a whole is
consistent — a small number of items are repeated so we can see whether ratings are stable
— but that is reported for the panel, never for a person, and it is a check on our study
design rather than on you.

## What will you record about me?

- A pseudonymous identifier such as `rater-03`. Not your name.
- A broad description of your background — for example "compliance analyst, 5–10 years" —
  so we can describe the panel honestly in the paper.
- For each item: your ratings, your yes/no answer, how long you were actively on the item,
  and the text before and after your edits.

We do **not** record your name, your employer, or anything about cases you have worked on
in your professional life, in the research dataset. Your contact and payment details are
held separately by [FINANCE/ADMIN FUNCTION] and are never joined to your responses.

**Please do not type anything confidential into the text boxes.** They are part of the
research record and are intended for publication. An automated scanner checks every edit
for things that look like real identifiers, and withholds anything it flags.

## Will my data be published?

We would like to publish the item-level responses — the ratings, the timings and the
edited texts — in a public repository, so that other researchers can check our statistics.
Before publication:

- your identifier is replaced with a fresh, unrelated label;
- any free-text comments you write are **deleted entirely**;
- submission timestamps are removed;
- edits flagged by the identifier scanner are withheld.

**This is a separate consent, and it is optional.** You can take part and decline to have
your data published. If you decline, your responses are used in our analysis and excluded
from the public release.

You can withdraw your data up to the point the anonymised dataset is published. After
that, it cannot be recalled. We will tell you the date once the analysis schedule is fixed.

## Will I be paid?

Yes — see the compensation note you were sent with this sheet. In summary: a fixed payment
based on a piloted estimate of the time required, at a professional hourly rate, paid on
completion; and if the work takes you materially longer than estimated, you are paid for
the additional time. If you stop part-way you are paid pro rata for what you completed.

## What are the risks?

Very low. The main one is that a repetitive several-hour task is tiring, which is why it
is self-paced and interruptible. There is no physical risk and no sensitive topic.

If you find that reading about financial crime is uncomfortable for any reason, you may
stop immediately, with no consequence.
